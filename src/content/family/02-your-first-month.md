## How much you'd like to see

Families are different, and we'd rather ask than assume.

Some want to read every visit note the same evening. Others would far rather we simply picked up the phone when something needed saying, and otherwise got on with it. **Both are completely fine, and neither is the "right" answer.**

### It isn't called Otter

The app you need is called **Access Care Planning** — look for the red circle. Searching the app store for "Otter" won't find it.

It's the care system we use rather than something we built ourselves, so it carries their name, not ours. That trips up almost everyone, so if you've already gone hunting and given up, that's why.

### Finding your device ID

Open the app and look at the very top of the login screen — there's a line of small text there. That's your **device ID**, and we can't register you without it. Screenshot that whole screen and email it over.

You'll have **view-only access** — you can see everything recorded, but not change it. If something looks wrong, tell us and we'll correct it properly, so there's a clear record of what changed and why.

Access is set up for up to three people, chosen by your relative or their representative, and can be removed at any time by asking any of us. You don't need to give a reason.

## What we can and can't share

This is the one that causes the most upset early on, so we'd rather explain it now than have it come as a surprise.

If your relative can make their own decisions, then legally those decisions are **theirs** — including what gets shared with you, and what care they'll accept. Being their son or daughter doesn't by itself give you the right to decide for them. When we check with them first, that isn't us being obstructive. It's the law, and it exists to protect them.

It cuts both ways: the same rules mean nobody can make decisions about your relative without proper reason either.

## Money, and help beyond us

Things families often don't realise they can claim. **None of these come to us** — we'd just rather you knew about them.

<div class="hc-grid als-grid-4 als-wide">
  <div class="hc-card"><strong>Attendance Allowance</strong><p>For people over State Pension age who need help with personal care. Not means-tested, and savings don't count against it.</p></div>
  <div class="hc-card"><strong>Carer's Allowance</strong><p>If you're caring for someone yourself for 35 hours a week or more.</p></div>
  <div class="hc-card"><strong>A Carer's Assessment</strong><p>Your council assesses <em>your</em> needs as a carer, separately from your relative's. Free, and rarely asked for.</p></div>
  <div class="hc-card"><strong>Council Tax reductions</strong><p>Several apply, including where someone has a severe cognitive impairment.</p></div>
</div>

There's more going on locally than most people expect, too — day centres, memory cafés, carer support groups, befriending and community transport. Ask us and we'll point you at what's running near you.

For what our own care costs, see [the cost of home care](/cost-of-home-care).

## If something isn't right

Tell us. Early and small is far easier to put right than late and large.

You won't cause trouble by raising something, and it will never affect the care your relative receives. If it's serious we'll come out and see you in person, including at weekends. And we'll tell you what changed as a result — that last part is the bit most people never get.
